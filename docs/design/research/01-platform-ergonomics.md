# 01 — Platform, ergonomics and interaction patterns for mobile web / PWA (2024–2026)

Researched 31 Oct 2026 for the Voyroo redesign. Tags: **[Evidence]** = research or measured data, **[Standard]** = official guideline or spec, **[Convention]** = common professional practice or reported platform behaviour. Source dates are in brackets.

## Summary

- **Targets.** Platforms set a minimum: WCAG AA requires 24 px, Apple's default is 44 pt, Google's is 48 dp. The research says go bigger. Hoober's data shows taps land within about 7 mm at the centre of the screen but only about 12 mm at the corners, and error grows when you're walking or riding. On a phone that's roughly 42–72 CSS px.
- **Thumb-zone charts are oversold.** People change grip all the time, and the middle of the screen is the easiest place to tap.
- **Navigation.** Use a visible, labelled bar of 3–5 destinations, for navigation only, not actions. Hidden navigation gets found about 20% less often.
- **Sheets.** One at a time, for short tasks only. Always show a Close button, let Back close the sheet, and never stack them.
- **Mobile web.** In 2026 that means safe-area insets on *both* iOS and Android (Android since Chrome 135), `svh` instead of `100vh`, inputs at 16 px or larger, and `visualViewport` for the keyboard (Safari still ignores `interactive-widget`). iOS push works only for Home Screen apps. iOS has no Vibration API.
- **Performance and offline.** Show cached content instantly with a "last updated" time, update optimistically with Undo, and retry quietly. No blocking spinners, no nagging banners.

## 1. Primary platform guidance

### 1.1 Apple HIG (Liquid Glass era, updated 2025–2026)
1. Controls default to 44×44 pt. 28×28 pt is Apple's *minimum*, not the target. Leave about 12 pt around bezelled controls and about 24 pt around borderless ones. [Standard] ([HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility))
2. Body text is 17 pt; never go below 11 pt. Large (default) Dynamic Type sizes: Large Title 34, Title 1–3 28/22/20, Body and Headline 17, Callout 16, Subhead 15, Footnote 13, Captions 12/11. Body reaches 53 pt at AX5. Support at least 200% text. [Standard] ([HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography))
3. Respect the safe areas (Dynamic Island, Home indicator). Put the most important content top and leading. At large text sizes, stack views and wrap text instead of truncating. [Standard] ([HIG Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout), Liquid Glass guidance added Jun 2025)
4. **Tab bars:** top-level navigation only, **never actions**. Keep the bar visible on top-level screens, give every tab a one-word label, never hide or disable a tab (show an empty state instead), and use badges only for critical items. iPad: 5 tabs or fewer. [Standard] ([HIG Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars))
5. **Sheets:** brief, self-contained tasks, not navigation. Use a medium detent (about half height) for progressive disclosure and a grabber on resizable sheets (the grabber works with VoiceOver). Support swipe-down, but **confirm if there are unsaved changes**. Pair Done with Cancel. **One sheet at a time.** [Standard] ([HIG Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets), updated Mar 2026)
6. Every gesture needs an on-screen button alternative. Under Reduce Motion, replace slides and zooms with fades. [Standard] ([HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility))
7. **Caution on Liquid Glass.** NN/g found that translucent controls floating over content hurt legibility, that collapsing and shifting tab bars fragment navigation, and that targets were under about 1 cm with 0.4 cm spacing. Their advice: keep navigation stable, contrast high, essential actions out of overflow menus, and motion meaningful. [Evidence/expert review] ([NN/g, Oct 2025](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/))

### 1.2 Material Design 3 (including Expressive, 2025)
1. **Navigation bar:** 3–5 destinations. With more than 5, use a rail. Expressive replaced the 80 dp bar with a shorter "flexible" bar (reported as 64 dp) and deprecated the navigation drawer. [Standard] ([M3](https://m3.material.io/components/navigation-bar/guidelines); [9to5Google, May 2025](https://9to5google.com/2025/05/14/material-3-expressive-navigation/))
2. **Targets** are at least 48×48 dp (about 9 mm), with at least 8 dp between them. [Standard] ([Android Accessibility Help](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858))
3. **FAB:** one per screen, for the screen's primary action. FAB 56 dp, new *medium* FAB recommended, large 96 dp, small 40 dp deprecated. Icon-only FABs need a label. [Standard] ([MDC FAB](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/components/FloatingActionButton.md))
4. **Bottom sheets:** a *standard* sheet has no scrim and coexists with the page. A *modal* sheet has a scrim and closes with Back, a tap outside, or a full drag down. The drag handle needs a 48 dp touch area, and assistive tech must be able to expand and collapse with it. Max width is 640 dp. [Standard] ([MDC BottomSheet](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/components/BottomSheet.md))
5. **Snackbars with an action** now persist until the user acts, for accessibility. Flutter 3.38 adopted this to match M3. [Standard] ([Flutter](https://docs.flutter.dev/release/breaking-changes/snackbar-with-action-behavior-update))

### 1.3 WCAG 2.2 (Oct 2023)
1. **2.5.8 Target Size (Min), AA:** 24×24 CSS px, or spacing such that a 24 px circle centred on each target doesn't touch any other target. [Standard] ([2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html))
2. **2.5.5 Target Size (Enhanced), AAA:** 44×44 CSS px. The rationale explicitly covers one-handed use and *users on shaking transit*, and recommends larger targets for frequent, hard-to-undo or hard-to-reach controls. [Standard] ([2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html))
3. **2.5.7 Dragging, AA:** anything done by dragging must also work with plain taps (reorder via up/down buttons, slider via tap on the track); a swipe doesn't count as the alternative. **2.5.1 Pointer Gestures, A:** path-based gestures need a single-tap alternative. [Standard] ([2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html))
4. **1.4.10 Reflow, AA:** no two-way scrolling at 320 CSS px. **1.4.4, AA:** text works at 200%. **1.3.4, AA:** don't lock orientation. [Standard] ([1.4.10](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html))
5. **2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured, AA:** sticky bars must never fully hide the focused element; use `scroll-padding` (C43). **2.4.13 Focus Appearance, AAA:** at least a 2 px perimeter with 3:1 change. [Standard] ([2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html), [2.4.13](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-appearance.html))
6. **4.1.3 Status Messages, AA:** toasts go through a live region. **3.3.7 Redundant Entry, A:** don't make people retype. [Standard]

## 2. Ergonomics: what the evidence says

1. **Grip varies and keeps changing.** Of 780 people observed touching phones, 49% held them one-handed, 36% cradled them and 15% used two hands. About 75% tap with a thumb, and people switch grip every few seconds. **Don't** design for one fixed thumb zone. [Evidence] ([Hoober 2013](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php); [Hoober 2017 Pt 1](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2017/03/design-for-fingers-touch-and-people-part-1.php))
2. **Accuracy depends on position:** about **7 mm at the centre** and about **12 mm at the corners**. Side list rows need to be 9–10 mm tall. One-handed use while carrying a bag can push far-corner error past 30 mm. Size targets about 20% larger than you think, because pt/dp-to-mm scaling varies by about ±30% between devices. [Evidence] ([Hoober 2017 Pt 3](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2017/07/design-for-fingers-touch-and-people-part-3.php))
   - Derived conversion: on current iPhones 1 CSS px ≈ 0.16–0.17 mm, about 6 px/mm. So 7 mm ≈ 42 px, 10 mm ≈ 60 px, 12 mm ≈ 72 px. **44 px only matches centre-screen accuracy for someone sitting still.**
3. **Studies Hoober cites:** error rises sharply below 15 mm and passes 40% under 8 mm (Henze). 10 mm buttons were hit 98–99.4% of the time (Lee and Zhai). *While walking*, 6.7 mm buttons had up to 23% errors, and about 20 mm targets removed nearly all of them (Schildbach and Rukzio). [Evidence] (same)
4. **Placement:** key actions go in the **middle half to two-thirds** of the screen. Secondary items go on the top and bottom edges, tertiary ones in corner menus. Keep irreversible actions away from frequently tapped controls, or make them undoable. Bars hold **at most 4 items, or 5 on the largest phones**. Make whole rows tappable. Pad the bottom of the page so the last item can scroll up to mid-screen. [Evidence-based guideline] ([Hoober 2017 Pt 2](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2017/05/design-for-fingers-touch-and-people-part-2.php))
5. **The bottom isn't automatically reachable.** NN/g calls reachability a "largely incorrect" reason for bottom sheets: the middle of the screen is easiest for most grips. Bottom bars work because they're always visible. [Evidence/expert review] ([NN/g, Jun 2023](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/))

## 3. Navigation, sheets, gestures, Back

1. **Visible beats hidden.** On mobile, a hamburger menu was used 57% of the time versus 86% for combined visible and hidden navigation. Discoverability fell by more than 20% and tasks were 15% slower. Show 4–5 or fewer top-level items as visible, labelled entries. [Evidence] ([NN/g 2016, n=179](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/); [NN/g 2015](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-navigation-patterns/))
2. **Sheets:** short interactions only, never complex content. Include a visible **Close** button (people overlook grab handles), let Back close the sheet, don't stack sheets, and don't replace page flows with them. [Evidence/expert review] ([NN/g 2023](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/))
3. **Accidental dismissal** is a top mobile problem. Prefer partial overlays to full-page ones, which people mistake for pages. Keep swipe-down but add an X. Treat system Back as undoing the overlay. [Evidence] ([NN/g, Sep 2022](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/accidental-overlay-dismissal/))
4. **Back:** people expect Back to go to whatever they *perceived* as the previous screen, including an overlay. 37% of sites tested got this wrong, and mobile users rely on Back more because corner X buttons are hard to hit. Use `<dialog>` or `CloseWatcher` so Android Back closes sheets (Chromium); add a history entry for full-height ones. [Evidence + Standard] ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/back-button-expectations); [Chrome 120](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-chrome-120))
5. **Swipe actions have no signifiers, so people don't find them.** Use swipe only for destructive actions, give it one meaning across the app, keep the item visible during the swipe, offer Undo, and always provide the same action as a visible button. [Evidence/expert review] ([NN/g 2017](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/contextual-swipe/))

## 4. Mobile web and PWA realities (Safari 26, Chrome 13x–14x)

1. **Viewport units:** `100vh` is taller than the visible area on load. Use `svh` for anything that must fit, `lvh` for backgrounds, and `dvh` only where resizing is acceptable (updates are throttled while toolbars move). All engines support them (Safari 15.4, Chrome 108). The keyboard doesn't change them. [Standard] ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/viewport-units))
2. **Safe areas on both platforms:** set `viewport-fit=cover` and use `env(safe-area-inset-*)`. Chrome on Android has drawn edge-to-edge since **Chrome 135 (2025)**, so bottom bars sit behind the gesture bar there too. **Don't** put `padding-bottom: env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` on bottom-anchored bars: it causes layout thrashing and stops Chrome's bottom bar ("chin") from sliding away. Use Chrome's `safe-area-max-inset-bottom` pattern. [Standard] ([Chrome edge-to-edge](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/edge-to-edge))
3. **Safari 26 ignores `theme-color`.** It tints its floating bars by sampling the body background and any fixed or sticky elements near the edges, so a coloured fixed bar or overlay can retint the browser chrome. Set `html`/`body` backgrounds deliberately and test on a real device. [Convention] ([Ben Frain, 2025](https://benfrain.com/ios26-safari-theme-color-tab-tinting-with-fixed-position-elements/))
4. **Keyboard:** iOS and Android (Chrome 108+) resize only the *visual* viewport, so fixed bottom bars end up under the keyboard. `interactive-widget=resizes-content` works in Chrome and Firefox but **not in shipping Safari**: it is in WebKit source since Sep 2026 but hasn't shipped. Use `visualViewport` events to keep a sheet's primary button visible, or hide the tab bar while a field has focus. [Standard + Convention] ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/viewport-resize-behavior); [Bramus, Sep 2026](https://www.bram.us/2026/09/11/webkit-supports-interactive-widget-and-hopefully-safari-will-too/))
5. **16 px inputs:** iOS zooms the page when a focused field is under 16 px. Use `font-size: max(16px, 1em)`. **Never** use `user-scalable=no`: it breaks WCAG, and iOS ignores it anyway. [Convention] ([Strahl 2023](https://weblog.west-wind.com/posts/2023/Apr/17/Preventing-iOS-Textbox-Auto-Zooming-and-ViewPort-Sizing); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/meta/name/viewport))
6. **Overscroll:** put `overscroll-behavior: contain` (Safari 16+) on sheets and panels so they don't scroll the page behind. iOS Home Screen apps have **no pull-to-refresh**, so provide your own refresh, or refresh when the page becomes visible. [Convention] ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/overscroll-behavior))
7. **Install:** on iOS 26, any site added to the Home Screen opens as a web app, and no manifest is needed. There's still **no install prompt on iOS**; people use Share › Add to Home Screen. Chrome fires `beforeinstallprompt` after at least one tap and 30 s of viewing. Promote install after a sign of real interest (joining a trip), not in the middle of a task. Make it dismissible, remember the dismissal, and show a snackbar promo for 4–7 s. [Standard] ([WebKit 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/); [install criteria](https://web.dev/articles/install-criteria); [promote install](https://web.dev/articles/promote-install))
8. **iOS push and badges** (16.4+) work **only for Home Screen apps**, and permission must come from a user tap. Declarative Web Push (iOS 18.4, Mar 2025) works without a service worker. [Standard] ([WebKit 2023](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/); [Declarative Push](https://webkit.org/blog/16535/meet-declarative-web-push/))
9. **No `navigator.vibrate()` on iOS.** The `<input switch>` workaround is non-standard. Haptics should only ever be an optional extra. [Convention] ([Ionic issue](https://github.com/ionic-team/ionic-framework/issues/29942))
10. **View Transitions:** same-document transitions are Baseline as of Oct 2025. Cross-document transitions work in Chrome 126+ and Safari 18.2+ but not Firefox. Use them as progressive enhancement and turn them off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. [Standard] ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available))
11. **Preferences:** `prefers-color-scheme`, `prefers-reduced-motion` and `prefers-contrast` work everywhere. `prefers-reduced-transparency` is **Chromium-only**. [Standard] ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-contrast))
12. **OS text size:** Chrome 146 (Mar 2026) adds `<meta name="text-scale" content="scale">`, which makes rem sizes follow Android's text-size setting. Don't also set a root `font-size`. On iOS the only hook is `font: -apple-system-body`. [Standard + Convention] ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/accessibility/respect-os-text-scale); [Vispero](https://vispero.com/resources/text-resizing-web-pages-ios-using-dynamic-type/))

## 5. Forms on mobile

1. Use `type=email/tel/url`. Use `type=text inputmode=numeric` for codes, **not** `type=number`. [Standard] ([web.dev forms](https://web.dev/articles/payment-and-address-form-best-practices))
2. Add `autocomplete` tokens (`name`, `email`, `tel`, `street-address`); WCAG 1.3.5 requires them for personal data. [Standard] ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/date-input/))
3. Set `enterkeyhint`: `next`, `done`, `search`, `send` or `go`. Baseline since 2021. [Standard] ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Global_attributes/enterkeyhint))
4. **Don't** use placeholders as labels. They vanish while typing, look like pre-filled data, have low contrast, and some screen readers skip them. Use persistent labels above fields. [Evidence] ([NN/g 2014/2018](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders/))
5. **Validate when the user leaves the field** or reaches the expected length, never on the first keystroke. Clear errors as soon as they're fixed. Don't disable Submit; say what's missing. 31–32% of sites have no inline validation. [Evidence] ([Baymard, Jan 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)) The evidence is mixed: two studies found immediate on-blur errors *increased* mistakes ([UX Movement](https://uxmovement.com/?p=10881)).
6. **Dates:** use native `type=date/time` for near-future dates; you get OS pickers and accessibility for free. Build a custom calendar only for ranges, and give each cell at least 44 px. Use three numeric fields for dates people already know. [Convention + Standard] ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/date-input/))

## 6. Performance as UX

1. **Core Web Vitals at P75 of field data:** LCP ≤ 2.5 s, **INP ≤ 200 ms**, CLS ≤ 0.1. "Poor" is above 4 s, 500 ms and 0.25. [Standard] ([web.dev, upd. May 2025](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds))
2. **2026 reference conditions:** a **Galaxy A24 4G-class** Android on **9/3 Mbps with 100 ms RTT**, plus 5/1 Mbps for wider reach. To load in 3 s, a JS-heavy page has about **1.2 MiB total, 0.62 MiB of it JS**. [Evidence] ([Russell, Nov 2025](https://infrequently.org/2025/11/performance-inequality-gap-2026/)) Lighthouse's mobile preset (150 ms RTT, about 1.6 Mbps, 4× CPU slowdown) is the usual lab stand-in. [Convention] ([Lighthouse](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/lighthouse/blob/main/docs/throttling.md))
3. **INP:** paint feedback in the next frame, then yield before heavy work. Keep the DOM small and use `content-visibility` on long lists. [Standard] ([web.dev INP](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-inp)) 0.1 s feels instant, 1 s keeps flow, and anything over 10 s needs progress and a way to cancel. [Evidence] ([Nielsen](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/))
4. **Loaders:** none under 1 s. A content-shaped skeleton only for *full-page* loads of 2–10 s. A spinner for a single module. A progress bar beyond 10 s. [Evidence/expert review] ([NN/g 2023](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/)) The research is mixed: Viget (n≈136) found skeletons felt *slowest* (2.82 s perceived versus 2.41 s for a spinner and 2.29 s for a blank screen); Mejtoft (2018) found the opposite. [Evidence] ([Viget 2017](https://viget.com/articles/a-bone-to-pick-with-skeleton-screens)) Rendering cached data beats any loader.
5. **Optimistic UI:** apply changes locally at once, sync in the background, show a "pending" mark only if sync is slow or fails, and prefer Undo over confirmation dialogs. Linear does this with a local replica and automatic retries. [Convention] ([Linear](https://linear.app/docs/offline-mode))

## 7. Offline and poor connectivity

1. **Say what still works**, not just that you're "offline", a word many people misread. Disable only the controls that need the network, and **never** show blocking modals on a shaky connection. [Standard] ([web.dev Offline UX](https://web.dev/articles/offline-ux-design-guidelines))
2. **Show freshness** ("Updated 14:05") on data that goes stale. Refresh automatically, but don't move content while someone is reading. [Standard] (same)
3. **Confirm local saves quietly.** Figma shows a small footer note that changes are saved locally and will sync. Notion (offline mode, 2025) has a per-page "Available offline" toggle and a sync indicator. Linear retries silently. [Convention] ([Figma](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040328553); [Notion](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/working-offline-in-notion-everything-you-need-to-know))
4. **Predownload what people will need, and say what's cached.** Google Maps refreshes offline areas on Wi-Fi within 15 days of expiry and states plainly what doesn't work offline. [Convention] ([Google Maps](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/6291838))
5. **Don't nag:** one small indicator while offline or while writes are pending, and per-item "Waiting to sync" marks rather than a red banner. Never use grey alone, which reads as "disabled"; pair it with an icon and text. [Standard + Convention] ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/offline-ux-design-guidelines))
6. **Retry** automatically with backoff, offer "Retry now", and retry when the page becomes visible. Detect being offline from failed requests, not `navigator.onLine`: `true` doesn't guarantee a working connection. [Convention] ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/onLine))

## 8. Accessibility on mobile beyond contrast

1. **Use native elements.** The bottom nav is a `<nav>` of links with `aria-current="page"`, not ARIA `menu` or `tablist` roles. [Convention] ([Roselli 2017](https://adrianroselli.com/2017/10/dont-use-aria-menu-roles-for-site-nav.html))
2. **Sheets are `<dialog>` + `showModal()`.** That gives you an inert background, a focus trap, Esc and Android Back closing, and focus restored on close. Use `autofocus` on the most likely first control, focus the least destructive button in a confirmation, name the dialog with `aria-labelledby`, include a visible Close button, and use `closedby="closerequest"` on sheets with input so a stray tap doesn't throw away typing. [Standard] ([APG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/); [MDN dialog](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/dialog))
3. **Every gesture has a button.** The web can't add VoiceOver rotor actions. [Standard] (WCAG 2.5.1/2.5.7; HIG)
4. **Toasts** use `role="status"`. An Undo toast must stay until a VoiceOver or Switch Control user can reach it (WCAG 2.2.1) and must not steal focus. [Standard]
5. **Test** at 200% text, at 320 px width, in landscape and with the keyboard open. Large text plus landscape plus keyboard can leave about 150 px of usable height, so sticky bars have to collapse. [Standard]

## Implications for an on-the-go trip app (Voyroo)

1. **Raise targets beyond 44 px.** DESIGN.md's 44 px fits a centre-screen tap by someone sitting still. Make Directions, Call, WhatsApp and Copy confirmation about 56–64 px with gaps of at least 8 px, and edge controls 64 px or more. Buses and boats are exactly the shaking-transit case WCAG describes.
2. **Put "Next"-card actions in the middle band.** Keep Delete away from them, and use Undo instead of confirmation dialogs.
3. **Reconsider "+" in the tab bar.** HIG says tab bars are for navigation, not actions. If "+" stays, it should open a sheet and look clearly different from the 4 navigation tabs (Hoober: at most 4–5 bar items).
4. **Sheets:** keep one at a time, add a visible Close button, let Back close them, and confirm before discarding typed input. Long editing belongs on a full screen, not in a sheet.
5. **Auto-focus only quick-add sheets.** The keyboard hides half the screen. Detail and edit sheets should open without it. iOS raises the keyboard only for `focus()` called synchronously inside the tap.
6. **Bottom bar:** use `viewport-fit=cover`, Chrome's max-inset pattern, `svh`, and `scroll-padding-bottom`. Hide the bar while a field has focus. Test Safari 26 tinting against the marigold ticket.
7. **Inputs:** at least 16 px, `inputmode=numeric` for confirmation codes, `tel` plus `autocomplete`, `enterkeyhint`, labels above fields, validation on blur, and native date and time pickers.
8. **Make Today offline-first.** Render from cache with "Updated 14:05", and precache the next day's addresses, phone numbers and confirmation codes.
9. **Keep sync status quiet:** one "Offline · changes will sync" chip, per-item "Waiting to sync" marks, automatic retry plus "Retry now". No red banners.
10. **Budget:** test on a Galaxy A-class phone at 9/3 Mbps and 100 ms RTT, and also at about 1–2 Mbps for boats. Aim for INP ≤ 200 ms and LCP ≤ 2.5 s.
11. **Loaders:** none under 1 s, a skeleton only on a first uncached load, never a full-screen spinner.
12. **Undo toasts** (already in place) need `role="status"` and should persist until the next action, not vanish after 3–4 s.
13. **Install and push:** show one dismissible install hint after someone joins a trip, with iOS Share-sheet steps. Ask for push permission only from a tap with a clear benefit, since iOS push needs a Home Screen install.
14. **Don't rely on vibration or translucency.** iOS has no Vibration API, and glassy bars over photos fail in sunlight. Keep bars solid and high-contrast, which Sunlight mode already does.
15. **Text and motion:** keep rem sizing, add Chrome's `text-scale` meta, test at 200%, 320 px and landscape, and gate View Transitions behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.
